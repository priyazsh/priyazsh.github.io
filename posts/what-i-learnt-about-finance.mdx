What I’ve Learned About Finances as a Student

I’m a student, and I don’t have a job right now, but I’ve earned money from many different sources.

My first earning was from a Discord bot called Karuta (only the OGs know it).

Since then, I’ve made money from various other sources too.

And one thing I’ve learned is that adult money is crazy.

You either spend it the right way or learn a lesson from spending it wrong.

Here’s what managing my own money has taught me so far.

Investing because someone introduced me to it

Since I’m still a student and my parents take care of my accommodation and basic needs, I had the opportunity to invest most of what I earned.

Someone introduced me to stocks and mutual funds, so I started investing. Over time, I managed to build a decent portfolio.

Looking back, I didn't know much when I started. I just trusted someone who knew more about it than me and started learning along the way.

Never lend money you expect to get back

If you lend someone money, there’s always a chance you’ll never see it again.

Sometimes people even start avoiding your calls, not just about the money, but entirely.

Of course, not everyone is like that. But it happens often enough that I learned one rule:

«Only give someone an amount you’re comfortable losing.»

Think of it as charity.

If they return it, great. If they don't, you don't lose your peace of mind.

Discounts can make you buy things you never needed

I’ve made plenty of impulse purchases.

Sometimes I bought something just because it was available at a discount. Other times, I bought random gimmicky things because they looked cool in the moment.

Some purchases were worth it. Many weren't.

So now I follow a simple rule:

«When you want to buy something, wait until the next day.»

If you still genuinely want or need it tomorrow, then consider buying it.

And when buying things from physical shops, I prefer using cash despite the convenience of UPI.

Physically seeing money leave your hand makes spending feel more real.

Never borrow money for a lifestyle you can't afford

I’ve never borrowed money just to maintain a lifestyle.

Borrowing money for things you don't actually need can make something cost much more than its original price.

I've seen people buy extremely expensive phones on long-term EMIs when all they really do on them is watch reels.

Personally, I'd rather spend that money on something like a good laptop — something I can use for work, learning, building, and potentially earning more money.

Your friend circle matters more than you think

One of the most valuable things I've learned about money actually came from a friend.

The same friend who introduced me to stocks and mutual funds also taught me about IPOs around a year ago.

I made a decent amount of money from that, and I'm always thankful to him for sharing that knowledge.

That's why I believe your circle matters.

The people around you influence what you learn, how you think, and sometimes even the opportunities you discover.

I'm still learning about money, and I'm sure I'll make more mistakes along the way.

But so far, these are some of the biggest lessons managing my own money has taught me.
